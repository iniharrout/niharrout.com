Why Build Custom When Off-the-Shelf ERPs Exist?

Off-the-shelf platforms like SAP, Oracle, or NetSuite charge exorbitant recurring license fees ($200–$500/user/month) and still require $100,000+ in rigid implementation consulting to adapt to non-standard manufacturing workflows. For growing industrial and manufacturing enterprises, licensing software that forces you to reshape your entire shop floor never pays off.

A tailored ERP scoped and built module-by-module belongs 100% to your enterprise — with zero per-seat licensing penalties, custom role permissions reflecting your actual hierarchy, and direct integrations with your factory floor and field teams.

What Drives the Cost Up or Down?

The final budget of an enterprise ERP is dictated by the depth of integration, departmental breadth, and security compliance required.

01 / Departmental Breadth & Modules

Number of Connected Business Units

Unifying two core departments (e.g. Sales CRM + Inventory) requires fewer data entities and approvals. Expanding scope to include complete HR payroll synchronization and complex P&L reconciliation adds sprint cycles for regulatory logic.

02 / Permission & Security Granularity

Role-Based Access Controls (RBAC)

Multi-tier organizations require precise authorization boundaries — from plant supervisors checking raw materials to CFOs approving vendor purchase orders and executive leadership viewing consolidated EBITDA.

03 / Hardware & Floor Verification

QR Check-in & Geolocation

Integrating browser-based mobile camera scanning, QR token validation, and GPS geo-fencing for shift clock-ins ensures shift integrity across physical plant sites without requiring expensive biometric hardware.

04 / Legacy Data Migration

Spreadsheet Ingestion & Sanitization

Legacy businesses often have years of disparate Excel sheets containing inconsistent customer names, SKU codes, and pricing tables. Automated data parsing, schema deduplication, and migration scripts influence initial timeline.

Rough Breakdown by Module & Phase

How a typical 4 to 9 month enterprise ERP engagement is staged in fixed two-week sprints.

Phase 01

Discovery, Data Schema & PRD

Cross-department stakeholder interviews, entity-relationship diagrams (ERDs), permission matrices, and detailed user flow specifications.

2 – 3 weeks
Est. 10% – 15% budget
Phase 02

HR Management & Access Controls

Attendance tracking, leave management, QR/mobile check-in, employee lifecycle workflows, and foundation role-based dashboards.

4 – 6 weeks
Est. 20% budget
Phase 03

CRM & Commercial Pipeline

Lead tracking, field visit logs, commercial quotation generation, automated discount approval hierarchies, and conversion metrics.

4 – 6 weeks
Est. 20% budget
Phase 04

Inventory & Supply Chain Engine

Multi-warehouse raw material tracking, minimum stock reorder triggers, automated Purchase Order (PO) creation, and vendor receipt logging.

5 – 7 weeks
Est. 25% budget
Phase 05

Finance, P&L & Month-End Close

Centralized cash flows, vendor invoice reconciliation, department cost centers, and automated financial statements reducing monthly close to 1 day.

4 – 6 weeks
Est. 15% – 20% budget
Phase 06

Executive Cockpit, QA & Plant Rollout

Real-time cross-functional leadership dashboard, data validation, stress testing under concurrent load, user acceptance testing (UAT), and plant deployment.

3 – 4 weeks
Est. 10% budget
